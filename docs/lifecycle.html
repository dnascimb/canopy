Canopy · workflow review

Moving a plant through its life

Every transition a plant makes and how the app performs it. Groups are containers: a plant on its own schedules, flips and harvests exactly like one in a batch. Written against the running app on 9 September 2026 — screenshots are the real screens.

The shape of it

Taking a cutting has no arrow here: it creates a new plant at clone rather than moving the mother anywhere. That it looks like a gap in the diagram is roughly how it feels in the app.

Long-flowering plants — hazes especially — skip veg and go from clone straight into flower. Indicas and hybrids veg first. Anything alive can be killed at any point, and a flowering plant can be sent back to veg.

Plant and group do the same thing

The schedule belongs to the plant. Its flip date is read off its lifecycle log — the last time it entered flower — and its length from its own run, falling back to the strain. A group has no dates of its own at all: it reports the span of whatever is inside it.

So moving one plant and moving a whole group are the same call. A plant with no group is not a lesser thing: it gets a flip date, a projected harvest, a bar on the timeline and rows in the event table, exactly like a group of one.

Every status change writes a row to the plant's lifecycle log, which is where its history — and the strip below — comes from. Nothing changes state silently.

The transitions

ten workflows
seed→seedling

Start a seed from the collection

  1. Plants → Add plant. Status seedling, pick the strain.
  2. Leave Location blank and it is placed by status — a seedling lands in the first vegetative space.
  3. Inventory → find the strain, press − to drop the seed count.

Step 3 is a different page and nothing links it to step 1. Starting a seed does not spend one, so the count drifts.

clone

Take in a clone from outside the collection

Same form, status clone, location set to your clone shelf. If the strain is new, add it first at Inventory → Add strain with seed type clone, so the inventory reads honestly: you hold a cutting, not seeds.

Nothing records that a plant came from outside. The only trace is what you type in its notes.

clone→flowering

Straight into flower — the haze path

Plant page → Move to → your flower space → Move. Moving into a flowering space sets the status to flowering on its own, because move_plants() aligns status with the destination's stage.

The plant starts its own run that moment: flip date today, harvest projected from its flower length, and a bar on the timeline. No group required.

clone→vegetative

Veg it first — the indica and hybrid path

Plant page → Move to → your veg space. Status becomes vegetative. For a whole batch use the group page, where a planned group also becomes vegetative.

vegetative→clone

Take a cutting off a veg plant

There is no action for this. Cloning is the most common thing you do and the app has no button for it. What you do instead:

  1. Plants → Add plant, same strain, status clone, location clone shelf
  2. Label it yourself so the parentage is guessable
  3. Write the parent into the notes, because nothing links them
  4. Log Took clones in the journal, separately

Once per cutting. Nineteen mothers cloned twice over is thirty-eight rows by hand.

vegetative→flowering

Flip to flower

Move all living plants to → your flower space. Identical to moving each plant by hand, because it is the same call: every plant starts its run and the group reports their span. Plants that were already flipped keep the date they had.

flowering→clone

Take a cutting off a flowering plant

Identical to taking one off a veg plant — the same Take a cutting button, on any plant still alive. The mother keeps the link after she comes down, so a harvested plant still shows what was taken off her.

flowering→harvested

Harvest

  1. Group page → Harvest → Record a harvest, with a date and notes, for the whole group or one plant. Everything it covers flips to harvested with an end date, and the group moves to drying once nothing is left in flower.
  2. Or cut one plant: Plant page → Set status → harvested. The rest keep flowering; the last one takes the group with it.

Recording the harvest is the harvest — there is no second step. No weights are kept: a harvest is a date, a target and notes. harvested is the plant's word for what the group calls drying; a plant has no separate drying status.

flowering→vegetative

Send it back to veg

Plant page → Move to → veg space. The plant leaves flower and the group's span shrinks to whatever is still flowering. Move them all and the group falls off the timeline on its own — there is no separate group date to clear.

any living→killed

Kill anything, any time

Plant page → Mark as killed, with a date and a reason. Killed plants stay on the record: out of living counts, capacity and strain summaries, but keeping their reason and shown struck through in the Markdown export. That is what makes the survival and loss reports mean anything.

Where it still falls back on you

two gaps

Cuttings are taken one at a time

A tray of thirty-eight is thirty-eight passes through the form. The link and the prefill are there; the bulk action is not.

Containers aren't modelled

A 16oz cup and a 3-gallon pot are identical to the planner, which is why 38 clones in a 2×2 block read as needing 66 sq ft. Backlog #16.

The plan

two done, two to go
done

A lifecycle event log

One append-only table: plant_id, on, from_status, to_status, space_id, note. Every move, status change, kill and harvest writes a row. Nothing else changes shape.

  • a real history per plant — clone 8 Jul → veg 18 Jul → flower 8 Sep, derived rather than typed
  • days in each stage, shown as the strip at the top of this page
  • flip dates that belong to the plant, which is what removed the plant-versus-group split entirely

Still to come from the same table: how long a strain takes from cut to harvest, averaged across runs.

done

Lineage links

Plant.parent_id records the plant a cutting came off. Take a cutting prefills the strain, status and mother, and both ends show the link. The Gorilla Snacks line — mother harvested, her clone in flower, a cutting off that clone on the shelf — is now three clicks rather than three notes.

next

Take N cuttings at once

How many, into which space, in one submission — creating the children with their parent set and their first event written. The only part of propagation still done a row at a time.

small

Say what an action will do

A line under the group's move control — will flip 7 plants and start their run today — costs nothing and removes the last surprise. Seed counts are deliberately left alone: they are a note to self, and nothing reads them.

The lifecycle log, the plant/group split and lineage links are built. Bulk cuttings and the action preview are not — they are written down here so the shape is agreed first. The walkthrough above describes the app exactly as it stands, on 9 September 2026.
